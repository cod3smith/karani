# Drafting a pack

<Lede>
The one step worth paying a good model for. It is also the step with the
strictest rule in the project: nothing karani says about you is allowed to
be something you did not say first.
</Lede>

```bash
karani draft 1482
```

## What a pack is

Four things, written in one pass so they agree with each other:

- **A tailored resume.** Not a rewrite — a *selection* over your own
  profile records, ordered for this role, with the bullets that do not
  argue for this job left out.
- **A cover letter**, in your register rather than a model's.
- **Answers** to the application's questions.
- **A keyword report**: which terms the posting uses that your resume does
  not, scored deterministically.

The output lands in `drafts/` as markdown, and as `.docx` and PDF in the
job's object-storage prefix when that is configured, with presigned links
that open on a phone ([ADR
0014](/decisions/0014-artifact-store-and-humanizer/)).

## The provenance rule

The tailorer does not take prose back from the model. It takes **records
with citations**, and verifies each one before anything ships:

| What came back | What happens |
|---|---|
| A citation naming a record that does not exist | Dropped |
| A quote that is not a verbatim span of your transcript | Dropped |
| A rewrite that lost a figure the source had | Falls back to the original |
| A record with no provenance at all | Discarded, not repaired |

That is mechanical, not a prompt asking nicely ([CLAUDE.md
4.13](https://github.com/cod3smith/karani/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)). The reason
is simple: a fabricated accomplishment gets through a resume screen and
then gets probed in an interview, and you are the one who has to answer.

A pack that comes out thinner because three claims could not be verified is
the correct outcome. The alternative is a pack that reads better and is
partly false.

## The attestations a model never writes

Work authorization, visa sponsorship, relocation and start date are not
drafted. They are **resolved** from the legal facts you declared in
`[legal]`, by code, and handed to the model as settled input:

> A drafting model may *receive* the resolved line. It must never produce
> one.

The same question has opposite correct answers depending on the role's
shape. *"Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?"* is **no** for a
global-remote role engaged through an employer of record and **yes** for
the Berlin role that sponsors a visa. A model that is right most of the
time is producing a misrepresentation on a signed form the rest of the time
([CLAUDE.md
4.12](https://github.com/cod3smith/karani/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)).

An unknown geography resolves to **needs review**, never to an assumed yes.

The same rule covers numbers you will state as fact. The comp figures in an
interview prep pack are derived from the posted band, your declared floor
and comparable postings karani has already ingested — the model may phrase
around them, it may never originate them.

## The humanizer

A draft goes through a detector and a rewrite pass. The detector is
deterministic and it **arbitrates**: it scores the text for the usual
tells, the rewrite addresses them, and the score is what decides whether
the rewrite was an improvement rather than the model's opinion of its own
work.

The Slack review card shows the voice score, so you can see whether the
pack you are about to approve reads like you.

## Keyword coverage

`karani/drafting/keywords.py` is deterministic — no model. It extracts the
posting's terms, finds the ones your resume does not use, and feeds that
gap into the draft prompt. The final coverage is persisted per application,
which makes it a *measurable* variable: `karani funnel` reports coverage
for applications that got a response against those that did not.

Not every missing keyword is worth adding. The gap is an input to the
prompt, not an instruction to stuff terms in.

## The review card

With Slack configured, each pack arrives as a card: the summary, the cover
letter, the voice score, links to the documents, and four buttons —
*Approve pack*, *Skip role*, *I applied (warm)*, *I applied (cold)*.

Approve marks it `ready` and links the posting. Then **you** submit on the
portal and press *I applied*. Every click records the verdict, feeds the
taste calibration, and updates the Notion board.

There is no button that submits, and there will not be ([ADR
0012](/decisions/0012-autopilot-draft-human-send/)).

## Unattended drafting

The hourly pass drafts packs for new top-fit roles by itself. Three bounds,
all of them necessary:

| Bound | Default | What it stops |
|---|---|---|
| Fit floor | 85 | Drafting for roles you would not apply to |
| Per-run cap | 3 | One strange hour emptying the budget |
| Daily cap | 5 | Twenty-four strange hours doing the same |

A drafted posting leaves the candidate pool, so no posting is ever drafted
twice. Setting the per-run cap to 0 disables drafting without disabling the
pass.

<Note kind="caution" title="The fit floor is on one model's scale">

85 means something different under each qualify model — measured over 598
rows, one model put 2 rows above 80 and another put 31 there. A floor tuned
against one and left alone after switching matches almost nothing, or
everything ([ADR
0022](/decisions/0022-conversion-ledger-and-variant-promotion/)).

</Note>

## Follow-ups

```bash
karani followup 1482
```

A note for an application that has gone quiet, hooked on something that has
actually happened at the company since you applied — from the cached
dossier (`karani intel <company>`), not from a template that says "I wanted
to follow up".
