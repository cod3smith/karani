# Vision and non-goals

<Lede>
karani is a personal tool, not a product. Knowing what it refuses to do is
most of understanding it.
</Lede>

## The problem

Applying to senior and staff engineering roles from outside the usual
locations is a signal-to-noise problem, not a discovery problem. The boards
surface thousands of postings a day. The intersection of *fully remote with
no country restriction*, *paid at San Francisco bands regardless of
location*, and *a role you are actually qualified for* is well under 1% of
the raw volume.

Finding that 1% by hand takes forty-five minutes a day and still misses
most of it. Five applications a week, maybe one screen for the effort.

karani inverts the shape: ingest broadly and cheaply, filter in tiers,
qualify against a real resume, draft the application. The goal is **fewer,
better-fit applications, better tailored, in less time** — not more
applications.

## Two bets

**Global-remote at San Francisco bands is a real and growing segment.** The
list of companies that hire globally at one published band is small but
shipping. If you can *reliably identify* those roles in the flood, the
funnel is dramatically better than the location-adjusted market. The whole
pre-filter exists to make that identification reliable.

**Models are cost-effective at the qualification tier, not the discovery
tier.** Running a model over every posting on every feed is expensive and
no better than arithmetic. Running one over the twenty that survived
arithmetic is cheap enough to do carefully. The tier split is the first
decision in the project and everything else sits on it ([ADR
0001](/decisions/0001-tiered-filter-not-single-llm-pass/)).

## The positioning axes

Every design decision traces to one of these:

| Axis | How it is treated |
|---|---|
| Global-remote vs region-locked | Region-locked is a veto, **unless** relocation is sponsored |
| SF bands vs location-adjusted | Location-adjusted lowers the score; below the floor where disclosed is a veto |
| Senior IC vs junior or manager | Junior is a veto; manager is allowed but scored lower |
| Engineering-adjacent vs everything else | Sales, marketing, design and operations are vetoed at the classifier |

The signals are named for what they detect — `global_hire`,
`regional_restriction`, `pay_parity` — and not for any candidate's country.
The filter is location-agnostic even though the motivation was not: a
US-only posting is vetoed wherever you live.

Role scope has widened once. Computational-biology **research engineering**
is in scope as of October 2026, where the work is building systems and
models rather than bench science; wet-lab titles stay excluded ([ADR
0021](/decisions/0021-computational-bio-is-in-scope/)).

## Who it is for

One person, and that is a design constraint rather than a limitation to fix:

- No multi-tenant assumptions anywhere in the schema. Single-user
  throughout.
- No admin UI. A CLI, an HTML console, generated markdown, and three
  conversational surfaces.
- No accounts, no auth, no sharing. Local files and a local database.

To use it yourself, fork it and rewrite `karani.toml`, `data/resume.md` and
probably the curated target list. The prompts are already de-personalised —
nothing about the candidate is hard-coded in them, it is rendered from
`[positioning]`.

## Non-goals

These are deliberate, so that effort does not get spent on them.

**It is not a job board.** No public site, no discovery experience for
anyone else.

**It is not an applicant-tracking system.** The state machine tracks your
applications, not a company's candidates.

**It will not submit applications.** Autonomy stops at a drafted pack and a
state transition. You press submit. Three reasons, and all three still
hold: some applications have terms about automated submission; one bad
model day becomes thirty broken applications, sent; and a recruiter's read
of a personal application is part of what you are sending ([ADR
0012](/decisions/0012-autopilot-draft-human-send/)).

**It will not scrape LinkedIn or Indeed.** Hostile to their terms, and
brittle. A company that only posts there gets added to the target list by
hand.

**It is not a general-purpose model harness.** Every prompt is
career-specific and lives in a versioned module.

**It is not a dashboard.** No charts. The terminal, one HTML file, markdown,
Slack.

## Success metrics

Real ones:

- **Time to shortlist** — minutes between "karani ran" and "I know what to
  apply to". Target: under 15.
- **Qualification precision** — the share of `qualified` rows you agree
  with, once the feedback loop has 30 or more pairs. Target: over 60%.
- **Application-to-screen conversion** — the baseline was about 1 in 5.
  Target: better than 1 in 3.
- **Model cost per applied role** — target under $2 all in, which the
  reference configuration beats by running qualification locally.

## Non-metrics

Deliberately not tracked, because optimising them makes the tool worse:

- Total postings ingested. More sources is not more value.
- Total qualifications run. Cost per *applied* role is what matters.
- Model eval scores. The verdict feedback loop is the eval.

## Where it stands

A personal tool with one user, published because the pieces are useful and
the decisions are worth reading. There is no hosted version and no plan for
one.
